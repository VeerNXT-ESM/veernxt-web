## The Universe and Origin Theories

The Universe represents the entire continuous expanse of space and time, housing all form of matter, energy, celestial bodies, and cosmic forces. It lacks a defined physical boundary and undergoes constant expansion. 

### Key Theories on the Origin of the Universe

*   **Big Bang Theory**: Proposed by Georges Lemaître in 1927 and later validated by Edwin Hubble’s observation of cosmic expansion in 1929. The theory states that approximately 13.8 billion years ago, all matter was concentrated into an infinitely dense and hot single point (singularity). A rapid expansion triggered the formation of space, time, subatomic particles, and eventually simple elements (hydrogen and helium).
*   **Steady State Theory**: Proposed by Hermann Bondi, Thomas Gold, and Fred Hoyle. It posits that the universe is homogenous, isotropic, and maintains a constant average density over time through continuous creation of matter as space expands.
*   **Pulsating Universe Theory**: Suggests that the universe undergoes infinite cycles of expansion and contraction (Big Crunch followed by another Big Bang).

## Galaxies and Stellar Structure

### Galaxies
A galaxy is a massive gravitationally bound system consisting of stars, stellar remnants, interstellar gas, dust, and dark matter.

*   **Milky Way Galaxy (Akash Ganga)**: A barred spiral galaxy containing our Solar System. Its center hosts a supermassive black hole named *Sagittarius A**.
*   **Andromeda (M31)**: The nearest major spiral galaxy to the Milky Way, located approximately 2.5 million light-years away.
*   **Types of Galaxies**: Classified by Edwin Hubble into three primary types:
    1.  *Spiral*: Possess a central bulge surrounded by rotating spiral arms rich in gas and young stars (e.g., Milky Way, Andromeda).
    2.  *Elliptical*: Feature an ellipsoidal shape with older stellar populations and low interstellar gas.
    3.  *Irregular*: Lack a distinct geometric structure, often formed by gravitational interactions.

### Stars and Stellar Evolution
Stars are luminous spheres of plasma powered by internal nuclear fusion reactions converting hydrogen into helium.

*   **Light-Year**: The distance traveled by light in one Julian year in a vacuum ($9.461 \times 10^{12} \text{ km}$).
*   **Astronomical Unit (AU)**: The mean distance from the Earth to the Sun ($\approx 149.6 \text{ million km}$).
*   **Parsec**: A unit of length equal to approximately $3.26 \text{ light-years}$ ($3.08 \times 10^{13} \text{ km}$).
*   **Chandrasekhar Limit**: The maximum mass limit for a stable white dwarf star, equal to approximately $1.44$ solar masses ($1.44 M_\odot$). Above this threshold, a dying star collapses into a neutron star or black hole.

## The Solar System

The Solar System consists of the Sun and the astronomical objects gravitationally bound to it, situated in the Orion Arm of the Milky Way galaxy.

### The Sun
The central star of the Solar System, comprising about 99.86% of its total mass. Primary components are Hydrogen ($\approx 73\%$) and Helium ($\approx 25\%$).

*   **Core**: The innermost region where nuclear fusion occurs at extreme temperatures ($\approx 15,000,000^\circ\text{C}$).
*   **Photosphere**: The visible surface layer of the Sun with an effective temperature of approximately $5,500^\circ\text{C}$ to $6,000^\circ\text{C}$.
*   **Chromosphere**: The lower atmosphere layer located above the photosphere, visible as a reddish glow during a total solar eclipse.
*   **Corona**: The outermost atmospheric layer consisting of low-density plasma, extending millions of kilometers into space. Visible during total solar eclipses.
*   **Sunspots**: Darker, cooler regions on the photosphere caused by strong magnetic field concentration. They follow an average cycle of 11 years.
*   **Solar Transit Time**: Light emitted from the Sun takes approximately 8 minutes and 20 seconds (500 seconds) to reach Earth.

## Classification of Planets

Planets in the Solar System are categorized into two groups based on structure, density, and atmospheric composition:

### Terrestrial (Inner) Planets
Composed primarily of rock and metals, possessing solid surfaces, higher densities, few or no natural satellites, and slow rotational speeds.
1.  **Mercury**
2.  **Venus**
3.  **Earth**
4.  **Mars**

### Jovian (Outer) Planets
Composed primarily of hydrogen, helium, methane, and ammonia, characterized by large diameters, low densities, rapid rotation, ring systems, and extensive natural satellite networks.
1.  **Jupiter**
2.  **Saturn**
3.  **Uranus**
4.  **Neptune**

## Physical Profiles of Planets

### 1. Mercury
*   Closest planet to the Sun and the smallest planet in the Solar System.
*   Lacks a substantial atmosphere, resulting in extreme temperature variations between day ($\approx 430^\circ\text{C}$) and night ($\approx -180^\circ\text{C}$).
*   Shortest orbital period around the Sun ($\approx 88$ Earth days).
*   Natural Satellites: 0.

### 2. Venus
*   Often termed "Earth's Twin" due to similarities in size, mass, and bulk composition.
*   Hottest planet in the Solar System ($\approx 465^\circ\text{C}$) due to a dense atmosphere composed of over 96% carbon dioxide, triggering a runaway greenhouse effect.
*   Exhibits retrograde rotation (rotates on its axis from East to West).
*   Known as the "Morning Star" or "Evening Star" due to its high atmospheric reflectivity (albedo).
*   Natural Satellites: 0.

### 3. Earth
*   Third planet from the Sun and the densest planet in the Solar System ($5.51 \text{ g/cm}^3$).
*   Only known celestial body to host liquid surface water and life.
*   Atmospheric composition: Nitrogen ($78\%$), Oxygen ($21\%$), Argon ($0.93\%$), Carbon Dioxide ($0.04\%$).
*   Axial tilt: $23.5^\circ$, responsible for seasonal variations.
*   Natural Satellites: 1 (The Moon).

### 4. Mars
*   Known as the "Red Planet" due to widespread surface iron oxide (rust).
*   Features **Olympus Mons**, the largest volcano and highest known peak in the Solar System, and **Valles Marineris**, an extensive canyon network.
*   Atmosphere consists primarily of carbon dioxide, nitrogen, and argon.
*   Natural Satellites: 2 (**Phobos** and **Deimos**).

### 5. Jupiter
*   Largest and most massive planet in the Solar System.
*   Features the **Great Red Spot**, a persistent high-pressure anticyclonic storm system.
*   Fastest rotational period among all planets ($\approx 10$ hours).
*   Natural Satellites: Features numerous moons, including **Ganymede**—the largest satellite in the Solar System, which is larger than Mercury.

### 6. Saturn
*   Second-largest planet, distinguished by its prominent, extensive planetary ring system composed mainly of ice particles, rocky debris, and dust.
*   Lowest density of all planets ($0.69 \text{ g/cm}^3$, less dense than water).
*   Natural Satellites: Features numerous moons, including **Titan**—the second-largest satellite in the Solar System and the only moon known to possess a dense atmosphere.

### 7. Uranus
*   Classified as an "Ice Giant" due to an atmosphere dominated by hydrogen, helium, water, ammonia, and methane. Methane absorption of red light gives the planet a blue-green color.
*   Possesses an extreme axial tilt of approximately $97.8^\circ$, causing it to rotate essentially on its side.
*   Exhibits retrograde rotation (East to West).
*   Natural Satellites: Includes **Titania**, **Oberon**, and **Miranda**.

### 8. Neptune
*   Most distant major planet from the Sun.
*   Appears deep blue due to high atmospheric methane concentrations.
*   Experiences the fastest atmospheric winds in the Solar System (reaching up to $2,100 \text{ km/h}$).
*   Longest orbital period around the Sun ($\approx 165$ Earth years).
*   Natural Satellites: Features moons including **Triton**, which orbits in a direction opposite to Neptune's rotation (retrograde orbit).

## Other Celestial Bodies

### The Moon (Earth's Satellite)
*   **Selenology**: The scientific study of the Moon's physical features, surface, and origin.
*   **Perigee**: The point in the Moon's orbit where it is closest to Earth.
*   **Apogee**: The point in the Moon's orbit where it is farthest from Earth.
*   Rotational and orbital periods are synchronously locked at approximately 27.3 days, causing the same face of the Moon to perpetually face Earth.
*   Lacks an atmosphere; gravitational acceleration is approximately $1/6\text{th}$ that of Earth ($1.62 \text{ m/s}^2$).

### Dwarf Planets
Celestial bodies that orbit the Sun and have sufficient mass for self-gravity to achieve hydrostatic equilibrium (nearly round shape), but have not cleared the neighborhood around their orbits.
*   **Pluto**: Reclassified from a major planet to a dwarf planet by the International Astronomical Union (IAU) in August 2006. Its largest moon is **Charon**.
*   **Ceres**: The largest object located in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
*   **Eris, Haumea, Makemake**: Trans-Neptunian dwarf planets located in the outer Solar System.

### Asteroids
Small, rocky, metallic bodies that orbit the Sun, predominantly located in the **Asteroid Belt** situated between the orbits of **Mars and Jupiter**.

### Comets
Cosmic bodies composed of ice, dust, and rocky material that originate from the **Kuiper Belt** or the **Oort Cloud**. When passing close to the Sun, solar radiation causes volatile materials to vaporize, releasing gas and dust that form a visible glowing atmosphere (coma) and a tail pointing away from the Sun.
*   **Halley's Comet**: A short-period comet visible from Earth every 75–76 years (last seen in 1986; next projected appearance in 2061).

### Meteoroids, Meteors, and Meteorites
*   **Meteoroid**: A small rocky or metallic piece of debris moving through interplanetary space.
*   **Meteor**: A meteoroid that enters Earth's atmosphere and vaporizes due to friction, generating a streak of light ("shooting star").
*   **Meteorite**: A meteoroid that survives its passage through the atmosphere and impacts Earth's surface.
